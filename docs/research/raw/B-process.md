## 4. Ranked Importance of Siting Factors (Industry Survey Data)

### Deliverable Power Now Dominates

- **Power availability outweighs all other factors.** "Deliverable power now outweighs land prices, incentives, and even fiber in site selection." — DataCenterKnowledge.com citing industry experts. [https://datacenterknowledge.com/data-center-site-selection/grid-constraints-steer-dutch-data-centers-beyond-amsterdam]

- **Power timing is the primary criterion.** "Site selection in 2026 is driven primarily by deliverable power. If developers cannot secure megawatts on a predictable timeline, incentives, land costs, and fiber connectivity become secondary." — Assad Noori (JLL EMEA), DataCenterKnowledge.com, Sep 2026. [https://datacenterknowledge.com/data-center-site-selection/grid-constraints-steer-dutch-data-centers-beyond-amsterdam]

### Time-to-Revenue Over Cost

- **Time to revenue now equals/exceeds cost priority.** "Cost and delivery timeline are now co-equal with availability in site decisions." — Assad Noori, JLL. [https://datacenterknowledge.com/data-center-site-selection/grid-constraints-steer-dutch-data-centers-beyond-amsterdam]

- "There's more of a focus on time to revenue than cost. What's not as easy to fix is time-to-revenue pressure or constraints." — Sean Farney, VP Data Center Strategy at JLL. [https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/data-center-site-selection/power-availability-now-determines-where-data-centers-get-built]

### Secondary Factors (When Power Constrained)

- Land costs, fiber connectivity, incentives, and climate/water availability drop to secondary when power is unavailable on predictable timelines. No ranked scoring methodology from industry surveys fetched.

### Uptime Institute 2026 Survey

- Uptime Institute's 16th Annual Global Data Center Survey (July 2026) was fetched but detailed results on location factor rankings were not accessible within preview windows. [https://www.uptimeinstitute.com/research/reports/annual-survey-2026]

## What Actually Kills Candidate Sites

### Primary Kill Factors (Fetched Sources)

1. **No deliverable power on acceptable timeline** — AI demand surge (30–60 MW → 300 MW single sites) overwhelms TSO capacity; transformer procurement delays (3 years) alone can sink projects dependent on grid timelines. [DataCenterKnowledge.com, Sep 2026]

2. **Grid congestion (Amsterdam case)** — TenneT capacity exhaustion forcing developers beyond Amsterdam, implying prior candidates were rejected due to grid unavailability. [DataCenterKnowledge.com, Sep 2026]

3. **Equipment lead times** — Transformer and electrical equipment shortages extend timelines beyond project windows; cost acceptable, schedule not. [JLL/DataCenterKnowledge.com, 2026]

### Secondary Kill Factors (Industry Job Descriptions Imply)

- **Permitting/zoning complexity** — Microsoft Land Acquisition role emphasizes "zoning, entitlement, legal risks" and "government engagement," but no data on how many candidates fail at permitting stage.

- **Water/cooling availability** — Industry references (e.g., "AI can't outprompt shortage of power, water, and land," The Register, Sep 2026 headline) suggest water constraints exist but quantitative failure rate not fetched.

- **Fiber connectivity** — Listed as secondary to power, but no failure rate data retrieved.
